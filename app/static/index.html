What it is

One shared memory for one person — medicines, routines, warning signs — kept in plain words everyone understands.

Why it exists

Care knowledge lives in one person's head. Aides change, family covers shifts, things get forgotten — and mistakes happen.

What it does

A 60-second brief before each shift, a nudge when a warning sign is due, and a simple view of who knows what.

  1. 1

    Tell it once

    Type a fact, send a voice note, or upload the discharge papers. Everything keeps its source — who said it, and when.

  2. 2

    Get a short brief

    Before a shift, each person gets a one-minute recap: what they're about to forget, plus anything that changed.

  3. 3

    Stay covered

    Warning signs are checked most often. If nobody reliably knows one, the family is alerted straight away.

Live from Ruth's circle — for each warning sign, a tick means someone in the circle reliably knows it.
